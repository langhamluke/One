# User Reviews of Saving / Budgeting / AI Money Apps Popular with Young Adults (Acorns, Stash, Qapital, Cleo, Rocket Money, Digit/Oportun, Chime) — as of 2026-10-01

Method note: apps.apple.com, trustpilot.com, nerdwallet.com and cashadvanceapps.com were blocked by the network proxy, so the numbers below come from search-result snippets and secondary review sites, not first-hand reads of the store listings. Treat every rating and count as approximate and check it against the live listing before publishing. Several sources are SEO or affiliate blogs (e.g., joingerald.com, unstar.app, strategia-x.com, checkthat.ai). They are flagged where used.

## 1. Ratings, review counts, and regulatory/enforcement themes

### Takeaway
App-store ratings are uniformly high (about 4.5–4.8 on iOS). Third-party complaint venues (Trustpilot, BBB, ConsumerAffairs) are much more negative, and they all point to the same few problems: fees, hard cancellation, surprise charges, and money stuck in accounts. The biggest enforcement actions are the FTC's $17M Cleo settlement (Mar 2025; refunds still unpaid as of late Sept 2026) and the CFPB's $2.7M Digit order (Aug 2022), which found that Digit's automatic savings caused overdrafts.

### Cited Findings
**Ratings (approximate, from snippets)**
- Acorns: 4.7 stars on the App Store from about 959K ratings; the company claims more than 14M customers served — [Acorns reviews page (company source)](https://www.acorns.com/reviews/), [Moneywise](https://moneywise.com/investing/reviews/acorns)
- Stash: 4.7/5 on the App Store (301K+ reviews) and 3.7/5 on Google Play (104K+ reviews) — [FinanceBuzz Acorns vs Stash](https://financebuzz.com/acorns-vs-stash) / [CreditDonkey](https://www.creditdonkey.com/apps-like-acorns.html) (snippet; date unclear)
- Qapital: 4.8/5 on the App Store (83K+ reviews) and 4.4/5 on Google Play (21K+ reviews) — same snippet sources as above
- Rocket Money: 4.48 on the App Store from 389,375 ratings and 4.53 on Google Play from 138,845 ratings (said to be as of Sept 2026). Trustpilot is about 3.5 stars from about 4,110 reviews. The source explains the gap this way: store reviews capture the first month, when finding subscriptions feels valuable, while Trustpilot captures the whole customer journey — [checkthat.ai (aggregator, lower reliability)](https://checkthat.ai/brands/rocket-money/reviews); also 4.5 iOS / 4.6 Play per [Inquirer USA](https://usa.inquirer.net/166883/rocket-money-reviews-and-ratings)
- Rocket Money has an A+ BBB rating, but BBB lists many closed complaints, mostly about billing and cancellation — [Ramsey Solutions](https://www.ramseysolutions.com/budgeting/what-is-rocket-money); [BBB complaints page](https://www.bbb.org/us/md/silver-spring/profile/billing-services/rocket-money-inc-0241-236043013/complaints)
- Cleo: one review aggregator says its recent average rating "dropp[ed] to 3.0 as of August 2026," mainly because of fees. The platform is unclear, so this is not a verified App Store figure — [cashadvanceapps.com user reviews](https://www.cashadvanceapps.com/user-reviews/cleo-user-reviews/) (search snippet). Trustpilot page: [meetcleo.com](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/meetcleo.com?page=2) (could not fetch)
- Oportun (formerly Digit) was named best savings app by Bankrate for 2024 and 2025 and by Forbes Advisor for 2024. It costs $5/month after a 30-day trial — [FinanceBuzz](https://financebuzz.com/save-with-digit), [Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/banking/savings/best-money-savings-apps)

**Enforcement and complaints (with dates)**
- **FTC v. Cleo AI, announced March 27, 2025: $17M settlement.** The FTC alleged that Cleo:
  - misrepresented how much money users could get in an advance;
  - falsely promised cash "today" or instantly, when advances actually took several days unless the user paid an express fee;
  - made cancellation hard and kept charging after repeated cancel requests;
  - told users they could not cancel until outstanding advances were repaid.

  The order requires clear disclosure of subscription terms, express informed consent before charging, and a simple way to cancel — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ftc-reaches-17-million-settlement-with-cash-advance-company-cleo-ai), [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/cleo-ai-agrees-17-million-settlement-ftc), [All About Advertising Law, Apr 2025](https://www.allaboutadvertisinglaw.com/2025/04/cleo-ai-settles-with-ftc-for-17-million-for-alleged-misleading-practices-and-autorenewal-violations.html)
- One source says that as of late Sept 2026 no Cleo refunds had been paid, and the FTC had not opened a refund program or named an administrator — [OpenClassActions (secondary)](https://openclassactions.com/news/cleo-ai-ftc-refunds-status-2026.php)
- **CFPB v. Hello Digit (now Oportun), August 2022:** a $2.7M civil penalty plus at least $68,145 in redress. The CFPB found that Digit:
  - misrepresented that its algorithm saved "the perfect amount" and would never overdraw users;
  - failed to reimburse all overdraft fees as promised;
  - kept interest on customer funds while saying it did not.

  The company had received about 70,000 overdraft complaints since 2017 — [CFPB consent order PDF](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_hello-digit-llc_consent-order_2022-08.pdf), [Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/cfpb-fines-fintech-digit-27m-faulty-algorithm-overdraft-oportun/629445/), [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/cfpb-hits-mobile-saving-app-digit-with-2-7m-fine-for-overdraft-charges) *(older issue: 2022)*
- **Acorns:** *Bingham v. Acorns Grow* (filed Feb 13, 2019, Orange County, CA) alleged that Acorns pulls caused overdraft/NSF fees at users' banks; it settled for $2.5M in 2021. A separate case, "In re Acorns Fee Litigation," alleged poor disclosure of how monthly fees were collected when the funding source had insufficient funds — [Pioneer Merger Corp S-4 (SEC filing)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1829797/000110465921084323/tm2116619-3_s4.htm), [LegalClarity](https://legalclarity.org/acorns-settlement-how-to-claim-your-share/) *(older: 2019–2021)*
- **Stash** complaints on ConsumerAffairs include:
  - a $100 transfer-out fee, and an account frozen after a failed transfer;
  - being told to relink a bank in order to cancel, then being charged $3 right away;
  - monthly charges continuing after the balance was zeroed ("that's what you signed up for") — [ConsumerAffairs Stash](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/robo-advisors/stash.html)
- I found no specific CFPB/SEC enforcement action against Stash in this pass — [search attempt](https://www.acorns.com/learn/investing/acorns-vs-stash/) (no relevant result)

### Inferences
- High store ratings combined with low Trustpilot/BBB scores fit a pattern: people rate the app early, when the onboarding "aha" lands (finding subscriptions, the first round-up), but the anger shows up later, around fees, cancellation, and getting money out.
- "Savings automation that overdraws you" and "subscription you can't cancel" are documented regulatory themes, not just anecdotes.

### Gaps
- I could not verify current App Store/Google Play figures directly (blocked). Cleo's exact store rating and count are unverified.
- I did not pull CFPB complaint database counts by company (no access to the database's query interface in this pass).
- No current (2025–2026) BBB ratings for Acorns, Stash, Qapital or Cleo.

## 2. Most loved features

### Takeaway
What users praise is automation that needs no willpower: round-ups, rules, and recurring or paycheck-split transfers. They also like goal "buckets." For Rocket Money, finding and cancelling subscriptions is the hook. For Cleo, the roast/hype personality is the hook, and some users say it changed their spending.

### Cited Findings
- Reddit users often praise Acorns Round-Ups for making investing effortless — [joingerald.com summary of Reddit (secondary)](https://joingerald.com/learn/saving--investing/acorns-reddit-worth-it-2026)
- Qapital's main draw is "set it and forget it" rules: more than 15 rule types, including Round-Up, Set & Forget (fixed daily, weekly or monthly amounts), and "Guilty Pleasure" (save whenever you spend at a store you want to cut back on). Users also like unlimited goals, shared/joint goals, and no minimum balance — [Savology](https://savology.com/qapital-review), [Money Crashers](https://www.moneycrashers.com/qapital-review/)
- Qapital claims its users save $1,500–$4,300 a year on average (company claim) — [Savology](https://savology.com/qapital-review)
- Chime: Round Ups plus "Save When I Get Paid" (automatically moves 10% of direct deposits of $500 or more) need no fee and no minimum. One reviewer built an emergency fund over six months with the 10% auto-transfer. Chime estimates about $400 a year from Round Ups for people who use the card twice a day — [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/money/chime-review), [Money Under 30](https://www.moneyunder30.com/banking/reviews/chime/)
- Cleo's roast mode: "Cleo helped me build a budget for the first time in my life. The roasting feature sounds silly but it actually makes me think before I spend $40 on DoorDash again." Users also like how easy it is to request an emergency advance — [cashadvanceapps.com user reviews](https://www.cashadvanceapps.com/user-reviews/cleo-user-reviews/), [FinanceBuzz Cleo](https://financebuzz.com/cleo-review)
- Rocket Money: users say it helps them track subscriptions, organize spending, and catch billing problems — [Inquirer USA](https://usa.inquirer.net/166883/rocket-money-reviews-and-ratings), [CNBC Select](https://www.cnbc.com/select/rocket-money-review/)
- Digit/Oportun: an algorithm decides "safe" amounts to move into savings; the company claims members save more than $1,800 a year — [FinanceBuzz](https://financebuzz.com/save-with-digit)

### Inferences
- The features users credit with changing behavior are the ones that take the decision out of their hands (rules, round-ups, paycheck splits), plus small moments of friction or reflection at the point of spending (the Cleo roast, Qapital's Guilty Pleasure rule).
- Goal-based buckets with names (e.g., "Spring break") look like a key retention feature, but I found no quantified evidence.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-company) data on how much round-ups or rules raise savings. All per-user savings figures are company claims.

## 3. Most hated features and problems

### Takeaway
Flat monthly fees that eat small balances top the list. Next come hard or ineffective cancellation, surprise charges (Rocket Money's bill-negotiation cut, Cleo's express fees), bank-link loops, overdrafts caused by automated pulls, and slow or costly withdrawals.

### Cited Findings
- **Fees on small balances.** Acorns charges $3, $6 or $12 a month (Bronze/Silver/Gold). $3 a month is 3.6% a year on a $1,000 balance. You need about $14,400 in Bronze to get under 0.25% — [Financer](https://financer.com/review/acorns/), [Forbes Advisor](https://www.forbes.com/advisor/investing/acorns-review/)
- On a $300 Acorns balance, $36 a year is about 12%. Reddit users say the fee "made sense at $500" but became a drag at $2K–$5K, and they moved to free brokerages — [joingerald.com (secondary)](https://joingerald.com/learn/saving--investing/is-acorns-worth-it-2026)
- Acorns charges $50 per ETF to transfer out, and users must zero the balance before cancelling — [Financer](https://financer.com/review/acorns/)
- Stash costs $1 to $9 a month, plus extra fees — [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/robo-advisors/stash.html)
- Qapital's move to paid tiers drew criticism ("New Pricing Plans Are Disappointing"). It has no retirement accounts and pays a low APY (0.05%) on Spending — [The College Investor](https://thecollegeinvestor.com/20472/qapital-review/), [Savology](https://savology.com/qapital-review)
- **Cleo fees.** A $5.99 or $14.99 membership is required before any advance. Same-day/express fees run $3.99–$9.99 per advance. One user: "you're paying $10+ per advance which defeats the whole point of 0% interest" — [LendEDU](https://lendedu.com/blog/cleo-app-review/), [Firstcard](https://www.firstcard.app/learn/cleo-cash-advance-review), [cashadvanceapps.com](https://www.cashadvanceapps.com/reviews-complaints/cleo-reviews-complaints/)
- **Rocket Money.** Users report:
  - bill-negotiation fees (35–60% of first-year savings) charged without knowing a negotiation was underway;
  - hard-to-cancel Premium, and charges after cancelling;
  - "surprise charges over $100" — [Wall Street Survivor](https://www.wallstreetsurvivor.com/is-rocket-money-worth-it/), [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/finance/truebill.html), [Inquirer USA](https://usa.inquirer.net/166883/rocket-money-reviews-and-ratings)
- **Bank-link breakage.** Rocket Money's negative reviews often describe a loop: the bank confirms the link, then the app asks for it again — [unstar.app analysis of 150 reviews (lower reliability)](https://unstar.app/blog/rocket-money-wont-link-bank-account-loop-reviews-2026)
- **Automation causing overdrafts.** Digit's roughly 70,000 overdraft complaints (2017–2022) and Acorns' overdraft class action (2019–2021) are the documented examples — see §1 sources.
- **Misleading marketing.** The FTC said Cleo's "instant" and "today" claims were false — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ftc-reaches-17-million-settlement-with-cash-advance-company-cleo-ai)

### Inferences
- For a student with a balance under $1,000, a flat $3–$15 monthly fee is the single biggest driver of "this app is costing me money" and quitting.
- Cash-advance products turn a savings app into a short-term credit product. That is where the regulatory risk and the worst reviews sit.

### Gaps
- No systematic Reddit thread counts. Reddit pages were not directly fetched; Reddit sentiment is relayed through secondary summaries.
- Customer-service and withdrawal-delay complaints are anecdotal; I found no quantified data.

## 4. What changed behavior, and what users ask for

### Takeaway
Users credit automatic, default-on transfers (round-ups, % of paycheck, recurring rules) and in-the-moment nudges (the Cleo roast) with changing their behavior. The most common asks are lower or no fees for small balances, easy cancellation, higher APY, and no overdraft risk. I found no reliable structured feature-request data.

### Cited Findings
- Paycheck split (10% auto-save) built an emergency fund "effortlessly" over six months — [Benzinga Chime](https://www.benzinga.com/money/chime-review)
- The Cleo roast "actually makes me think before I spend" — [cashadvanceapps.com](https://www.cashadvanceapps.com/user-reviews/cleo-user-reviews/)
- Users who moved off Acorns went to zero-fee brokerages (Fidelity, Vanguard). This points to unmet demand for free or percentage-based pricing at low balances — [joingerald.com (secondary)](https://joingerald.com/learn/saving--investing/is-acorns-worth-it-2026)
- Qapital is criticized for low APY and no retirement accounts, which implies users want a better yield and a retirement option — [Savology](https://savology.com/qapital-review)
- Digit's "no overdraft" promise was central to its marketing; the gap between that promise and reality drove complaints — [CFPB order](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_hello-digit-llc_consent-order_2022-08.pdf)

### Inferences (synthesis, not verified data)
- Likely requested or missing features:
  - free tier or fee waiver under a balance threshold;
  - one-tap cancellation (which the FTC order now requires of Cleo);
  - overdraft guardrails, i.e., a "don't pull if checking falls below $X" setting;
  - competitive APY on goal buckets;
  - reliable bank links.
- Gamified and personality features (roast, streaks) seem to help early engagement. Automation (rules) seems to sustain saving.

### Gaps
- No app-store "feature request" corpus was accessible. Direct Reddit and App Store review text could not be fetched.

## 5. Retention and churn evidence

### Takeaway
Personal finance apps lose most users within 30 days. The benchmarks vary widely (about 11–38% Day-30 retention) and come mostly from vendor blogs. Users quit because of fees on small balances, cancellation and billing frustration, broken bank links, and manual-entry effort.

### Cited Findings
- Apptopia's 2024 report reportedly puts Day-30 retention for personal finance apps at 38% (62% churn). Other benchmarks put fintech Day-30 retention at 11.6% or 15–22% — [Plotline](https://www.plotline.so/blog/retention-rates-mobile-apps-by-industry), [UXCam](https://uxcam.com/blog/mobile-app-retention-benchmarks/), [semnexus](https://semnexus.com/day-1-day-7-day-30-retention-benchmarks-app-category-2026) (benchmarks conflict)
- One source cites Sensor Tower 2024 data that the top 10 personal finance apps lose 71% of daily active users between Day 1 and Day 30. The same source says manual-entry apps lose users 3x faster than auto-sync apps — [strategia-x.com (low reliability; primary data not verified)](https://www.strategia-x.com/blog/2026-04-12-why-budgeting-apps-fail-30-days-fintech-ux-data/)
- That same source also says a "CFPB 2024 Financial Wellbeing Survey" found 67% of people who tried budgeting apps rated them not helpful or too much effort. **I could not verify this, and it may be fabricated or mis-attributed. Do not use it without checking the primary source.** — [strategia-x.com](https://www.strategia-x.com/blog/2026-04-12-why-budgeting-apps-fail-30-days-fintech-ux-data/)
- The divergence between store ratings and Trustpilot ratings suggests dissatisfaction builds after the first month — [checkthat.ai](https://checkthat.ai/brands/rocket-money/reviews)

### Inferences
- Churn triggers, ranked by how often the sources mention them (synthesis):
  1. fee is larger than the gains on a small balance;
  2. billing or cancellation anger;
  3. overdrafts caused by automatic pulls;
  4. bank-link breakage;
  5. effort to maintain (manual budgeting).

### Gaps
- No company-disclosed retention figures for Acorns, Stash, Qapital, Cleo or Rocket Money.
- No primary source for the Sensor Tower or CFPB figures above.

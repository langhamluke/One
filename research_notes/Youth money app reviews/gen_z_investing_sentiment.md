# Gen Z Sentiment on Investing and Money Apps (US), as of 2026-10-01

Method note: about 15 web searches. Several primary sources (finra.org, finrafoundation.org, fortune.com, finance.yahoo.com) were blocked by the network proxy, so some figures below come from search-result snippets of those pages or from secondary coverage. Treat them as "snippet-verified": the URL is the original, but I could not read the full page. Reddit threads did not show up directly in search, so Reddit sentiment is a gap.

## 1. Current ratings and main complaint themes (Robinhood, Webull, Public, Cash App) plus 2025–2026 regulatory actions

### Takeaway
Every app shows the same split. App-store ratings are high (4.1–4.8), while Trustpilot/BBB ratings are very low (1.2–1.7). The complaints are about the same things across apps: accounts frozen or restricted with no explanation, slow withdrawals, and support that is hard to reach or bot-only. Regulators have mostly pursued fraud, AML, and support failures rather than gamification. The SEC withdrew its gamification (digital engagement practices / predictive data analytics) proposal in June 2025. The newest pressure is on Robinhood's sports event contracts.

### Cited Findings
**Ratings**
- Robinhood: Apple App Store 4.3/5 (4.7M+ reviews); Google Play 4.1/5 (~525K reviews); Trustpilot 1.2/5 (~3,500 reviewers). Webull: App Store 4.7/5 (~333K); Google Play 4.6/5 (~211K); Trustpilot 1.3/5 (<375 reviewers). Source is the 2026 comparison page, as summarized in search results — [StockBrokers.com Webull vs Robinhood 2026](https://www.stockbrokers.com/compare/robinhood-vs-webull)
- Robinhood's Trustpilot score conflicts between pages. Search snippets show "1.2 / 5 Bad" on some regional/paged URLs and "3.9 / 5 Great" on the main US page — [Trustpilot US](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/robinhood.com); [Trustpilot (1.2 variant)](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/robinhood.com?b=MTYxODU4Mzc2MTAwMHw2MDc5YTBkMWY4NWQ3NTA4NzA2MGUxYzI). **Conflict, unresolved.** The difference may come from Trustpilot's handling of invited reviews or from caching. Check live before quoting.
- Public.com: Trustpilot 1.7/5 from only 19 reviews. Complaints on Trustpilot and BBB centre on withdrawal issues and an inactivity fee — [NerdWallet Public review 2026](https://nerdwallet.com/reviews/investing/brokers/public)
- Cash App: 4.7 Google Play and 4.8 Apple App Store; 100M+ installs — [unstar.app 2026 (aggregator, lower reliability)](https://unstar.app/blog/is-cash-app-legit-safe-scam-reviews-2026). BBB complaint profile: [BBB Cash App](https://www.bbb.org/us/ca/oakland/profile/mobile-apps/cash-app-1116-919908/complaints); [Trustpilot Cash App](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/cash.app)

**Complaint themes**
- Robinhood Trustpilot complaints:
  - Accounts restricted with no explanation.
  - Withdrawal delays (one user says the account was locked after requesting a $4,900 withdrawal flagged as "unusual activity").
  - Bot-only support ("no correspondence with a human being").
  - The Gold card was advertised as having no annual fee, but users say they were charged a $50 Gold subscription.
  - Slow fraud and dispute resolution.
  - Source: [Trustpilot Robinhood](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/robinhood.com)
- Cash App: an aggregator analysis says about 30% of 1-star reviews concern accounts closed or deactivated with no reason given — [unstar.app (aggregator)](https://unstar.app/blog/is-cash-app-legit-safe-scam-reviews-2026)
- Public: market data is paywalled; options tools are weak (no Greeks or bid/ask detail); no streaming quotes; no custodial accounts; adding a beneficiary needs emailed forms — [StockBrokers.com Public review](https://www.stockbrokers.com/review/public-com)

**Regulatory actions**
- **SEC withdrew** the 2023 predictive data analytics / digital engagement practices ("gamification") proposal on June 12, 2025, along with 13 other proposals — [Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/alert/sec-withdraws-fourteen-rule-proposals); [Vedder Price](https://www.vedder.com/insights-events/sec-withdraws-numerous-proposed-rules/)
- IOSCO issued a final report on digital engagement practices (FR/07/2025) — [IOSCO](https://www.iosco.org/library/pubdocs/pdf/IOSCOPD794.pdf)
- **FINRA–Robinhood (March 2025)**: $26M fine plus $3.75M restitution ($29.75M total), with no admission of wrongdoing. Findings:
  - AML failures, including missed account takeovers by hackers and missed manipulative trading.
  - Clearing-technology supervision failures.
  - Restitution covers customers whose collared market orders were cancelled and then re-entered at worse prices.
  - Sources: [FINRA release](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2025/finra-orders-robinhood-financial-pay-375-million-restitution); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250307774863/en/FINRA-Orders-Robinhood-Financial-to-Pay-$3.75-Million-in-Restitution-to-Customers-Fines-Robinhood-Financial-and-Robinhood-Securities-for-Anti-Money-Laundering-Supervisory-and-Disclosure-Violations)
- **CFPB–Block/Cash App (January 2025)**: $175M total, split into up to $120M in refunds and a $55M penalty.
  - Findings: weak security; incomplete investigations of unauthorized transfers; for years, no live customer service agents.
  - Requirement: 24-hour live-person support.
  - Source: [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-orders-operator-of-cash-app-to-pay-175-million-and-fix-its-failures-on-fraud/)
- A reported July 2026 multistate (46-state) settlement with Cash App alleged that it implied bank-like protections and opened accounts without basic identity details. This appears **only on low-reliability aggregators** — [unstar.app](https://unstar.app/blog/is-cash-app-legit-safe-scam-reviews-2026). **Unverified.** It may be confused with the January 2025 multistate money-transmitter settlement. Needs a primary source.
- **Massachusetts–Robinhood gamification case (settled January 2024, older)**: $7.5M. Robinhood agreed to permanently stop using confetti; the complaint also cited digital scratch tickets, free-stock rewards, push notifications, and "most popular" lists aimed at young, inexperienced users — [Yahoo/Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/robinhood-settles-massachusetts-gamification-case-165454008.html); [WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/regulation-compliance/robinhood-pays-7-5m-to-settle-massachusetts-gamification-charge)
- **Robinhood prediction markets / sports event contracts (2026)**:
  - Connecticut issued a cease-and-desist on sports event contracts (September 2026); Missouri ordered operators to stop.
  - Robinhood does not offer them in MD, MI, NV, or NJ.
  - A New York bill would ban purchases by anyone under 21.
  - Event-contract revenue was $156M in Q2 2026, more than 10x year over year; 13.6B contracts were traded.
  - Sources: [Covers timeline](https://www.covers.com/betting/prediction-sites/guides/prediction-markets-timeline); [Predscope](https://predscope.com/guide/robinhood-prediction-markets) (secondary, snippet-level)

### Inferences
- App-store ratings mostly reflect the everyday experience of using the app, which people like. Trustpilot, BBB, and CFPB complaints reflect what happens when something goes wrong (money stuck, account frozen, no human support). The trust gap sits in support and account-action transparency, not in the UI.
- With the SEC rule withdrawn, the US has no federal gamification rule. Enforcement is state-led (MA in the past, CT, MO, and NY on event contracts now). This leaves room for an app that limits risky nudges by its own choice and says so.

### Gaps
- I could not find a current CFPB complaint-database count for Robinhood, Webull, or Public.
- BBB letter grades were not retrieved.
- I found no current 2026 Webull or Public regulatory actions.
- The Robinhood Trustpilot conflict is unresolved.

## 2. What young investors praise and criticize

### Takeaway
They praise zero commissions, fractional shares, a clean mobile UI, IRA matches, and (on Public) no PFOF. They criticize gamified nudges toward options and crypto, opaque account restrictions, bot-only support, limited asset selection (no mutual funds or bonds on Robinhood), paywalled data, and past data breaches. Fidelity is the common "grown-up" alternative recommended to young investors.

### Cited Findings
- Robinhood's IRA match is 1% of contributions, or 3% for Gold members. Options trading is commission-free, while Fidelity charges $0.65 per contract — [NerdWallet Fidelity vs Robinhood 2026](https://www.nerdwallet.com/investing/learn/fidelity-vs-robinhood)
- Robinhood has no mutual funds or bonds and some OTC/foreign gaps. Fidelity offers zero-expense-ratio index funds and stronger support. Robinhood has had "multiple data breaches" — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/investing/learn/fidelity-vs-robinhood)
- Public users like the clean app, news feed, quick stock context, broad asset menu, low or zero costs, and **no-PFOF stance** — [NerdWallet Public](https://nerdwallet.com/reviews/investing/brokers/public); [Finder Public 2026](https://www.finder.com/stock-trading/public-review)
- Public's AI "Generated Assets" idea tool and 24/5 trading are marketed as differentiators — [StockBrokers.com Public](https://www.stockbrokers.com/review/public-com)
- Gamification criticism (confetti, scratch tickets, push notifications, top-movers lists) comes from the MA case — [WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/regulation-compliance/robinhood-pays-7-5m-to-settle-massachusetts-gamification-charge)
- Young investors take more risk:
  - Under-35 investors are much more likely to trade options (43% vs 10% of those 55+) and buy on margin (22% vs 4%).
  - Willingness to take substantial risk among under-35s fell from 24% (2021) to 15% (2024).
  - Crypto consideration among young adults fell from 32% to 26%.
  - Source: FINRA Foundation NFCS Investor Survey (2024 data, published Nov 2025) — [FINRA Foundation PDF](https://www.finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2025-11/NFCS_Investor_Survey_Report_White_Paper.pdf) (snippet-verified; full PDF blocked)
- Schwab 2025: 37% of investors use fractional shares, 28% use automated investing, and 32% use direct indexing; younger investors experiment the most — [Schwab Modern Wealth Survey 2025](https://www.aboutschwab.com/schwab-modern-wealth-survey-2025)

### Inferences
- Gen Z values the low-cost, low-minimum features that Robinhood made popular, but these are now standard (Fidelity, Schwab, and Public all have them). What sets apps apart now is trust (support, transparency, no PFOF) and breadth (index funds, bonds).
- Options and margin use remains high among under-35s even though their stated risk appetite is falling. Users say they want restraint, but the apps keep making risky products easy to reach.

### Gaps
- No primary Reddit thread quotes were retrieved, so I cannot cite r/investing, r/Bogleheads, or r/personalfinance sentiment directly.
- No data was found on complaints about confusing tax forms (1099 corrections) for 2025–2026.
- I found no specific 2025–2026 Robinhood outage incidents.
- SoFi Invest and Webull praise/complaint detail is thin beyond ratings.

## 3. Survey data: what Gen Z wants from money apps (finfluencers, education, privacy, motivators)

### Takeaway
Gen Z relies heavily on social media and finfluencers but says it does not fully trust them. They overrate their own knowledge, and many report losing money to bad online advice. They say they want credit-score education, budgeting and spending visibility, strong security and privacy, and trustworthy guidance from parents or professionals. AI is rising as an advice source.

### Cited Findings
- **FINRA Foundation (NFCS 2024 data, published Nov 2025; brief April 2026):**
  - Among investors under 35, 61% use social media for investment information and 61% follow recommendations from influencers or trading-app communities.
  - YouTube is used by 61% of under-35s.
  - Social-media/finfluencer users averaged 42% correct on an investment knowledge quiz, yet 63% rated their own knowledge as high.
  - Sources: [FINRA Foundation 2025 white paper](https://www.finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2025-11/NFCS_Investor_Survey_Report_White_Paper.pdf); [FINRA 2026 news release](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2026/finra-foundation-research-examines-characteristics-behaviors-outcomes); [FINRA Foundation April 2026 brief](https://finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2026-03/FINRA_Foundation_Research_Brief_Social_Media_Finfluencers.pdf) (snippet-verified)
- **Gen Z distrusts TikTok but uses it anyway:**
  - A 2026 study reports that Gen Z rates TikTok the least trustworthy place for financial advice yet still uses it.
  - Almost 1 in 5 lost more than $1,000 to bad online advice, and 21% lost $250–$1,000.
  - Sources: [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/gen-z-says-tiktok-least-160235529.html); [Benzinga, "70% take money advice from social media"](https://benzinga.com/z/45540111) (snippet-verified; underlying survey sponsor not confirmed)
- **Motley Fool:** TikTok is Gen Z's #2 source for dividend-investing education (47%), compared with 11% of Gen X — [Motley Fool Generational Investing Trends](https://www.fool.com/research/what-are-gen-z-millennial-investors-buying/)
- **AI as an advisor:** a September 2026 Fortune piece reports Gen Z trusts AI with stocks and sports bets while distrusting AI on jobs — [Fortune, 2026-09-18](https://fortune.com/2026/09/18/gen-z-ai-investing-advisors-financial-planning-sports-betting/) (page blocked; headline only)
- **Bank of America Better Money Habits 2025** (Ipsos, April 4–25, 2025; ages 18–28):
  - 72% took steps to improve their finances; 51% saved more; 24% paid down debt.
  - 66% are currently saving.
  - 33% are stressed about finances. When stressed, 69% check their balance and 64% make a budget.
  - 39% get family financial help.
  - Source: [BofA newsroom](https://newsroom.bankofamerica.com/content/newsroom/press-releases/2025/07/confronted-with-higher-living-costs--72--of-young-adults-take-ac.html)
- **Schwab Modern Wealth 2025:**
  - 71% of Gen Z are confident in their investment strategy, mainly because they learned early.
  - 62% say they are more patient now.
  - 39% have a documented financial plan (vs 26% of boomers).
  - Gen Z's "comfortable" threshold is $329K.
  - Source: [Schwab](https://www.aboutschwab.com/schwab-modern-wealth-survey-2025)
- **Credit and education wants:**
  - 58% of Gen Z want to understand how credit scores work; 42% want credit-building help from their financial institution.
  - 28% learned about credit from social media.
  - Sources: [Academy Bank survey](https://www.academybank.com/learn/media/in-the-news/academy-bank-survey-reveals-insights-into-gen-z-financial-habits-credit-knowledge-and-challenges); [Forbes Council 2026](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesfinancecouncil/2026/03/23/gen-z-is-rewriting-the-rules-of-financial-engagement-what-banks-should-know/) (attribution of individual stats between these two is mixed in snippets)
- **Security and privacy:** 57% of Gen Z prioritize identity and credit protection when choosing a bank; 50% want data protection — [snippet; source among Forbes Council / Academy Bank results above, attribution unconfirmed](https://www.forbes.com/councils/forbesfinancecouncil/2026/03/23/gen-z-is-rewriting-the-rules-of-financial-engagement-what-banks-should-know/)
- **App features Gen Z uses most:** paying bills (68%), moving money between their own accounts (59%), P2P transfers (54%), tracking spending (53%) — [Academy Bank](https://www.academybank.com/learn/media/in-the-news/academy-bank-survey-reveals-insights-into-gen-z-financial-habits-credit-knowledge-and-challenges)
- **Real-time visibility and budgeting tools** are among the most requested features; Gen Z uses mobile apps plus credit as a discipline tool — [PYMNTS 2026](https://www.pymnts.com/consumer-insights/2026/gen-z-turns-mobile-apps-and-credit-into-financial-discipline/)
- **CNBC|Generation Lab (May 2024, older; n=1,033, ages 18–34):** 42% were not saving or investing; only 17% were investing in stocks; 63% believe in building wealth through the stock market (Nov 2023 wave) — [CNBC 2024](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/05/07/the-job-market-is-poor-according-to-a-majority-of-young-americans-polled-in-the-cnbcgeneration-lab-youth-money-in-the-usa-survey-.html); [CNBC 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/07/more-than-half-of-young-americans-believe-in-building-wealth-through-the-stock-market-according-to-cnbcgeneration-lab-youth-money-in-the-usa-survey-.html)

### Inferences
- The most cited unmet need is trustworthy, accurate education delivered inside the app where money is moved. Gen Z wants it, currently gets it from social media, knows that is unreliable, and pays for it (losses above $1,000 for about 1 in 5).
- Credit literacy and credit building rank as high as investing, so an app focused only on investing misses a core want.
- Security and privacy are table stakes and the top selection factor. The Cash App and Robinhood enforcement history shows why this generation is wary.
- Motivators that appear across sources are visible progress (balance checking, budgeting, documented plans) and early learning (Schwab). These point to goal- and progress-based motivation rather than trade-count gamification.

### Gaps
- Experian, Morning Consult, and Cornerstone Gen Z 2025–2026 money-app surveys were not retrieved.
- I found no direct survey data on Gen Z's desire for social features in investing apps, or on willingness to share data for personalization.
- The 2026 TikTok-trust study sponsor and sample size are unconfirmed.

## 4. Signs that "learning + doing in one place" is valued; portfolio and diversification tools requested

### Takeaway
Incumbents are bundling learning and doing for young users. Fidelity Youth (ages 13–17) has its learning center inside the trading app, and Schwab finds early learning drives Gen Z confidence. Usage of diversification tools (fractional shares, automated investing, direct indexing) is meaningful. I found no direct survey that measures demand for a combined learn-and-invest product.

### Cited Findings
- Fidelity Youth Account (ages 13–17):
  - Teen-owned, with parental visibility; no fees, no minimums, $1 fractional shares, and a debit card.
  - Now integrated into the main Fidelity app with a Youth Learning Center curriculum covering goals, spending, saving, and investing, with "tools and approachable content all in one place."
  - Source: [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/go/youth-account/overview)
- Schwab 2025 links Gen Z's investing confidence (71%) mainly to learning about investing at an early age — [Schwab](https://www.aboutschwab.com/schwab-modern-wealth-survey-2025)
- Investment-method usage: buy and hold 56%, growth 53%, fractional shares 37%, direct indexing 32%, socially responsible investing 31%, automated investing 28% — [Schwab 2025 press release](https://pressroom.aboutschwab.com/press-releases/press-release/2025/2025-Modern-Wealth-Survey-Shows-American-Investors-Are-Expanding-Beyond-Traditional-Portfolios-to-Further-Diversify-and-Help-Find-Success-in-Todays-Market/default.aspx)
- 65% of Gen Z college students want to learn more about personal finance, and 50% expect to need help with budgeting and saving after graduation — [snippet, from Gen Z banking search results; likely Academy Bank or The Financial Brand](https://thefinancialbrand.com/news/gen-z-banking/generation-z-wants-it-all-and-wants-it-now-but-they-also-need-help-191074)
- The knowledge-confidence gap among social-media investors (42% correct vs 63% self-rated high) shows that education is needed where decisions are made — [FINRA Foundation](https://www.finrafoundation.org/sites/finrafoundation/files/2025-11/NFCS_Investor_Survey_Report_White_Paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Learn + do" is becoming standard among incumbents (Fidelity Youth) rather than a new idea. A new entrant would need to stand out on quality and safety, for example lessons tied to the user's real portfolio or nudges toward diversification instead of trading.
- The diversification tools young users already use (fractional shares, automated or theme investing) suggest demand for simple diversification features, such as visual concentration checks or one-tap diversified baskets. I found no survey that asks for these directly.

### Gaps
- No survey directly measures Gen Z demand for integrated education and investing, or for specific portfolio-analysis tools (concentration warnings, rebalancing, risk scores).
- I found no Reddit evidence on which portfolio trackers or budgeting tools young users request. Monarch, Copilot, and YNAB post-Mint sentiment was not researched.

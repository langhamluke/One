# Parent-Managed Kids/Teen Card Apps: User Reviews of Greenlight, Acorns Early (formerly GoHenry) and BusyKid

Research date: 2026-10-01. Method note: the network proxy blocked direct fetches of Trustpilot, Apple App Store, BBB, ConsumerAffairs, JustUseApp, FinanceBuzz and Kikaroo. Every finding below therefore comes from **search-engine snippets and summaries of those pages**, not from pages I read in full. Treat the exact numbers as approximate and re-check them on the live pages before publishing. Several secondary sources are **competitor-owned** and may be biased: greenlight.com reviews Acorns Early, busykid.com compares itself with Greenlight, and pennytime.app and kikaroo.app are rival kids-money apps. Each is flagged where it is cited.

## Q1: Current ratings and review counts (App Store, Google Play, Trustpilot, BBB, CFPB)

### Takeaway
Greenlight has very high app-store ratings (about 4.7–4.8, hundreds of thousands of reviews) but only a middling Trustpilot score (about 3.8) and a BBB "B". Acorns Early rates well (App Store about 4.6, Play about 4.5, Trustpilot about 4.2). BusyKid has the weakest app-store ratings (about 3.4 iOS, 3.7 Play) but a good Trustpilot score (about 4.3). For all three, app-store stars run well ahead of complaint-site sentiment.

### Cited Findings
**Greenlight**
- App Store 4.8 with "over 400,000" reviews; Trustpilot 3.8 with "nearly 6,000" reviews (summary of a June 2026 review) — [TopConsumerReviews](https://www.topconsumerreviews.com/best-personal-finance-software/reviews/greenlight.php); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/greenlight.com)
- App Store 4.8 and Google Play 4.7 "across tens of thousands of reviews" (2026) — [Kikaroo, a competitor blog](https://kikaroo.app/blog/greenlight-debit-card-review/)
- BBB accredited since December 2017, currently rated **B**; Trustpilot 3.8 with 5,500+ reviews — [FinanceBuzz](https://financebuzz.com/greenlight-review). The BBB profile (Greenlight Financial Technology, Atlanta) has at least 9 pages of complaints — [BBB complaints](https://www.bbb.org/us/ga/atlanta/profile/financial-services/greenlight-financial-technology-inc-0443-27762270/complaints?page=9)
- App Store review listing — [Apple](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/greenlight-for-families/id1049340702?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)

**Acorns Early (formerly GoHenry)**
- Trustpilot 4.2 from 3,965 reviews; App Store 4.6 from about 9,400 reviews; Google Play 4.5 from about 3,070 reviews — [Finder](https://www.finder.com/kids-banking/gohenry-kids-debit-card-app). The German Trustpilot mirror shows 3,677 reviews, so the count varies by date and locale — [Trustpilot DE](https://de.trustpilot.com/review/acorns.com/early)
- The App Store listing (id6566186346) is a **new app** created for the rebrand, so its review count is far below GoHenry's old one — [Apple](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/acorns-early-kids-money-app/id6566186346?see-all=reviews); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.acorns.early&hl=en_US)

**BusyKid**
- App Store 3.4 from about 1,500 reviews; Google Play 3.7 from about 2,100; Trustpilot 4.3 from 2,102 — [Finder](https://www.finder.com/kids-banking/busykid-prepaid-card); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/busykid.com)
- BBB: not accredited, rated **A-**, 1/5 stars from only 2 reviews, 7 complaints in the last 3 years — [BBB](https://www.bbb.org/us/az/scottsdale/profile/prepaid-credit-cards/busykid-1126-1000076301/complaints)

**CFPB**
- I found no summary of CFPB complaints for any of the three. Searches only turned up BBB, Trustpilot and Reddit dispute complaints — [Finder Greenlight](https://www.finder.com/greenlight-card)

### Inferences
- The gap between app-store and Trustpilot/BBB scores probably reflects in-app rating prompts that reach happy daily users, while complaint sites draw people with billing, fraud or closure problems.
- Acorns Early's low review count is partly a side effect of the new listing: GoHenry's review history did not carry over.

### Gaps
- Exact live ratings as of 2026-10-01 could not be checked because the pages were blocked.
- No CFPB complaint counts or themes were found. Next step: query the CFPB database for "Greenlight Financial Technology", "Acorns Grow" and "BusyKid".
- I found no BBB rating for Acorns Early or Acorns Grow.

## Q2: Most praised features

### Takeaway
Across all three apps, parents praise **chore and allowance automation, real-time spending visibility and controls, and ease of use**. Greenlight is praised for its polish and controls. BusyKid is praised for cheap investing in real stocks and for responsive support. Acorns Early is praised for chores, allowance and Money Missions.

### Cited Findings
- Greenlight: users find it "a great tool for teaching financial responsibility", "easy to use and effective for managing allowances, chores, and savings" (Trustpilot summary) — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/greenlight.com); [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/finance/online-banks/greenlight.html)
- Greenlight: parents get real-time alerts every time the card is used and can choose which stores the card works at. Kids can send a request to spend outside those limits — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/software-services/greenlight-review)
- Greenlight Reddit discussion is "overwhelmingly positive", though threads often ask whether a normal bank account would do the job — [LogicalDollar](https://logicaldollar.com/greenlight-review/)
- Acorns Early: custom chores with set payouts, recurring automatic allowance, 100+ Money Missions (videos and quizzes), spend limits, category blocking, card lock/unlock — [Greenlight's review of Acorns Early, a competitor source](https://greenlight.com/learning-center/reviews/acorns-early-review); [Beelinger](https://beelinger.com/acorns-early-review/)
- Acorns Early: relatives can send money through Giftlinks / Relatives accounts, a feature kept from GoHenry — [GoHenry blog](https://www.gohenry.com/us/blog/news/gohenry-is-becoming-acorns-early)
- BusyKid (Trustpilot summary): reviewers like it for teaching financial literacy, saving and investing, for its ease of use, and for motivating kids to do chores. They "consistently praise the customer service", citing prompt, knowledgeable and helpful support — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/busykid.com)
- BusyKid: chores are paid to the card every Friday. About $48/year covers up to 5 cards, with commission-free access to 4,000+ stocks and ETFs. Finder calls investing "rare among kids' cards" at this price. Relatives can add money for a $1 fee — [Finder](https://www.finder.com/kids-banking/busykid-prepaid-card)
- BusyKid: many Trustpilot reviews describe an issue that was "quickly solved" — [Finder summary of Trustpilot](https://www.finder.com/kids-banking/busykid-prepaid-card)

### Inferences
- Chore-linked pay and parental visibility are the core reasons families use these apps. Investing is a differentiator for BusyKid; Greenlight and Acorns put it behind higher tiers.

### Gaps
- I could not pull verbatim, dated App Store review quotes; the fetches were blocked.

## Q3: Most common complaints

### Takeaway
**Greenlight:** subscription cost and silent recurring billing, slow customer service (especially on the cheapest tier), painful fraud and dispute resolution, trouble closing accounts and getting money back, and cards not arriving or locking. **Acorns Early:** the GoHenry migration brought higher fees, glitches (allowance not paying, especially on Android), login and sync problems, card declines, and slow refunds after cancelling. **BusyKid:** the least-loved app experience (bugs, Plaid bank-linking errors), nickel-and-dime fees, slow card delivery, and some refund problems after cancelling, including a dated 2026 BBB case.

### Cited Findings
**Greenlight**
- Parents were charged monthly fees for long periods "without receiving monthly billing receipts or clear email notifications" — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/greenlight.com); [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/finance/online-banks/greenlight.html)
- Complaints about difficulty closing accounts and getting funds returned. Support response on the Core tier "can be slow" — [FinanceBuzz](https://financebuzz.com/greenlight-review)
- "Major complaints focus on high monthly fees, poor customer service, technical bugs, and card reliability issues" — [Kikaroo, a competitor source](https://kikaroo.app/blog/greenlight-debit-card-review/)
- Disputes are "a pain": dozens of Reddit, Trustpilot and BBB complaints about resolving fraudulent charges. Replacement cards never arrived, and cards were locked on receipt — [Finder](https://www.finder.com/greenlight-card)
- Hacked kids' cards, with reimbursement taking over a month; spending allowed over set limits; money moved without permission; merchant refunds not posting — [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/finance/online-banks/greenlight.html); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/greenlight.com)
- Price: tiers run about $5.99 to $19.98/month, so even the cheapest plan is about $72/year — [Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/credit-cards/reviews/greenlight-debit-card/); [LogicalDollar](https://logicaldollar.com/greenlight-review/)

**Acorns Early**
- Migration timeline: Acorns acquired GoHenry in April 2023. The US brand was folded into Acorns Early in November 2024 — [PennyTime, a competitor source](https://www.pennytime.app/learn/blog/what-happened-to-gohenry-gohenry-is-now-acorns-ea/); [GoHenry announcement](https://www.gohenry.com/us/blog/news/gohenry-is-becoming-acorns-early)
- Price went from about $4.99 per child per month (GoHenry) to $8/month for Early Lite (up to 4 kids), or included with Acorns Gold at $12/month. Gold adds Early Invest, a UGMA/UTMA account with a 1% match (as of July 2026) — [PennyTime](https://www.pennytime.app/learn/blog/what-happened-to-gohenry-gohenry-is-now-acorns-ea/); [Greenlight review of Acorns Early](https://greenlight.com/learning-center/reviews/acorns-early-review). Whether this is cheaper or dearer depends on how many kids a family has.
- Long-time user, paraphrased: they had used GoHenry since beta with no problems, but since the Acorns buyout "there are monthly fees, glitches, and money gets lost in cyberspace". Reviewers also complain that previously free features were removed — [Finder](https://www.finder.com/kids-banking/gohenry-kids-debit-card-app); [PennyTime](https://www.pennytime.app/learn/blog/what-happened-to-gohenry-gohenry-is-now-acorns-ea/)
- Glitches such as allowance not paying, "especially with the Android app". Funds not syncing, login problems, and no streamlined way to move money from the parent account back to a personal bank account — [Finder](https://www.finder.com/kids-banking/gohenry-kids-debit-card-app)
- A parent could not get money back for over a month after cancelling — [Finder](https://www.finder.com/kids-banking/gohenry-kids-debit-card-app)
- "Numerous reports of cards not working at various locations, widespread login and technical glitches", plus frustrating support, refunds and activation — [Greenlight review of Acorns Early, a competitor source](https://greenlight.com/learning-center/reviews/acorns-early-review). A 2026 review says the app "still glitches, though it's better than before" — [Beelinger](https://beelinger.com/acorns-early-review/)

**BusyKid**
- Complaints about payment processing, transaction discrepancies, account problems, a "buggy app" and slow card delivery (Trustpilot summary) — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/busykid.com)
- Repeated Plaid errors when linking a bank account. Fees include 50 cents per declined transaction, $5 to reissue a card, $5/month for paper statements and $1.50 per ATM withdrawal. There is no way to avoid the subscription — [Finder](https://www.finder.com/kids-banking/busykid-prepaid-card)
- BBB complaint: a customer cancelled on 3/8/2026, was owed about $230, and got no refund or reply after three emails — [BBB](https://www.bbb.org/us/az/scottsdale/profile/prepaid-credit-cards/busykid-1126-1000076301/complaints)

### Inferences
- Money-movement failures (disputes, refunds, cancelling and withdrawing balances) drive the most severe complaints for all three apps. These fintechs run through partner banks and their support is light, so cash stuck in limbo is the dominant pain point.
- The Acorns Early complaints are concentrated in the period after the 2024 migration, and the app is reportedly improving in 2026, though the evidence for that is thin.

### Gaps
- I could not separate older complaints from 2026 ones on Greenlight's Trustpilot, because review dates were not visible in the snippets.
- There is no quantitative breakdown of complaint categories.

## Q4: Missing or wished-for features, and the teen view

### Takeaway
I found only indirect evidence of feature requests. Implied wishes include: lower or free tiers; more kids per plan (Acorns caps it at 4); interest or cash back on kids' savings (Acorns Early lacks these); easy withdrawal of money back to the parent's bank; credit building (Greenlight partly answers this with its Family Cash Card); and more spending freedom for teens. A survey shows teens want independence, but I found **no teen-written reviews** of these specific apps.

### Cited Findings
- Acorns Early is "less ideal" for families that want a free or lower-cost card, more than four kids, interest or cash back on kids' savings, or family-safety features (location sharing, which Greenlight offers) — [Greenlight review of Acorns Early, a competitor source](https://greenlight.com/learning-center/reviews/acorns-early-review)
- Acorns Early has no streamlined way to move money from the parent app back to a personal bank account — [Finder](https://www.finder.com/kids-banking/gohenry-kids-debit-card-app)
- Greenlight debit alone does not build credit. Parents can add teens as authorized users on the Greenlight Family Cash Card — [LogicalDollar](https://logicaldollar.com/greenlight-review/)
- Teen attitudes (Wells Fargo survey, reported in the press; the survey date is not stated in the snippet): nearly 70% of teens say they should be able to spend their own money without restrictions, against 44% of parents. Nearly 9 in 10 teens find parents reading their texts scarier than parents seeing every purchase — [TechRadar / search summary](https://www.techradar.com/pro/software-services/greenlight-review); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/better/lifestyle/teen-debit-cards-real-world-way-teach-your-kids-about-ncna1071256)
- Reddit threads often discuss whether older kids should just get a normal (teen) bank account instead — [LogicalDollar](https://logicaldollar.com/greenlight-review/)

### Inferences
- The "outgrowing it" pattern is likely: teens with jobs probably move to bank-issued teen checking accounts, which are free and come with full debit cards. The evidence for this is indirect (Reddit "alternatives" discussions); I found no direct teen quotes.
- The low BusyKid app ratings may partly come from kids rating the app, but that is unverified.

### Gaps
- I could not reach r/teenagers, r/Parenting or r/personalfinance directly; I found no verbatim teen comments about feeling "babied".
- I found no structured lists of feature requests (for example, from developer replies on the App Store).

## Q5: Are financial-education features used or valued?

### Takeaway
Education content is everywhere in marketing: Greenlight's Level Up (launched in January 2023) and Acorns Early's 100+ Money Missions. Parent testimonials and reviewer write-ups are positive, but I found **no independent usage data**. Review summaries that describe what users love center on chores, allowance and controls, not lessons.

### Cited Findings
- Greenlight launched Level Up, a gamified curriculum (earning, saving, investing, credit, taxes) with XP, coins and badges, in January 2023 — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230106005027/en/Greenlight-Reinvents-Financial-Education-with-Level-Up-a-Gamified-Financial-Literacy-Curriculum-for-Kids-and-Teens)
- One parent quote, on Greenlight's own case-study page (promotional): "It's animated, colourful, and packed with super helpful financial lessons" — [Greenlight case study](https://greenlight.com/learning-center/case-studies/level-up)
- An independent reviewer calls the Level Up lessons "well developed" and says they "should help instill some financial savvy" — [Fioney](https://fioney.com/greenlight-level-up-review/)
- Acorns Early's 100+ Money Missions are carried over from GoHenry — [GoHenry blog](https://www.gohenry.com/us/blog/news/gohenry-is-becoming-acorns-early)
- The Trustpilot summaries for Greenlight and BusyKid name "teaching financial responsibility/literacy" as a theme of praise, but they credit it to the real-money experience (chores, saving, investing), not to lesson modules — [Trustpilot Greenlight](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/greenlight.com); [Trustpilot BusyKid](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/busykid.com)

### Inferences
- Parents seem to value "learning by doing" (earning, spending limits, savings goals, real stock purchases) more than formal lesson content. The education modules look like a nice-to-have rather than a reason people choose an app.

### Gaps
- I found no completion or engagement figures for Level Up or Money Missions, and no Reddit or app-store reviews that mention them either way.
